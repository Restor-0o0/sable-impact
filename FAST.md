# `fast` branch

The same mod as `release`, tuned for the tick rather than for the result.

Note what is *not* here: `optimize.batchBounds` and `optimize.cacheChunks` are on in both branches. They are
not trades — they cost nothing and change nothing about what breaks — and the first of them saves more on a
big crash than everything in the table below put together.

Nothing here is a code change any more. Every trade below is a config default, so this branch and the
release branch compile to the same behaviour given the same config file — what differs is what a fresh world
starts out with. Anyone running `release` can have any of these by editing
`<world>/serverconfig/create_aeronautics_impact-server.toml`, and anyone running this branch can have the
release behaviour back the same way.

Each item is a deliberate trade: the saving is real and so is what it costs.

| Setting | Release | Here | Saved | Paid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `blockUpdates` | `true` | `false` | The single largest cost in the mod. A full neighbour update in this pack is ~0.6 ms a block; this is a write and a packet. | Nothing tidies up after the hull. Torches on a bored tunnel float, sand over a crater does not fall, redstone beside it does not recalculate, until something else pokes them. |
| `movingCrushInterval` | `1` | `4` | The crush pass is a column scan plus ~10 probes for every block near the build, and it is the most expensive thing in the sweep. Quartered for anything moving. | A boulder rolling across a forest rides the treetops for a couple of tenths of a second at a time before the canopy under it gives. |
| `maxContactsPerTick` | `0` (unlimited) | `2048` | A hard ceiling on contacts examined per tick. `maxBlocksPerTick` capped destruction; nothing capped the looking. | In a very heavy pile-up the contacts past the ceiling are not examined, so not broken. The hull is still stopped by them — Sable resolves a contact this mod declines — it just chews through more slowly while the pile-up lasts. |
| `sweepFinestDetail` | `0` | `1` | Rewind sampling drops from 2.0 to 1.45 samples per block travelled on every carve. | The probe cube widens from 0.40 to 0.45 to keep the samples overlapping, so holes come out a little rounder and a little wider than the thing that made them. Nothing gets through — the pair moves together. |
| `backingMemoTicks` | `1` | `4` | A hull with thousands of contacts a tick pays for the look at what holds terrain up once every four ticks instead of every one. | A reading can be four ticks stale exactly where terrain is changing fastest, so a block whose hillside just went still reads as backed and holds one pass longer than it should. |
| `maxQuietTicks` | `20` | `60` | A parked or high-flying build is looked at once every three seconds rather than once a second. | Ground generated under a hovering hull, or a tower built up to meet it, goes unnoticed for up to three seconds. |
| `debris.mode` | `FALL` | `SETTLE` | The largest saving in the debris path and one of the largest in the mod. No falling block is created at all, so nothing ticks, falls, writes a block on landing or is tracked by every client in range — a crash becomes block changes and nothing else. | Nothing visibly comes off a wreck. The hull is there and then it is a heap; there is no moment of it falling. This is the single most obvious difference between the two branches. |
| `debris.maxSettlePerTick` | `256` | `128` | The ceiling on the path that `mode = SETTLE` sends everything down, so on this branch it is the ceiling on the whole of debris. Halved. | A wreck leaves a thinner heap under a big landing, and takes a few more ticks to finish putting it down. |
| `debris.scatterChance` / `debris.contraptionScatterChance` | `0.25` / `0.3` | `0.15` / `0.2` | A piece of debris is a ticking entity that has to fall, land, write a block back and be sent to every client in range. This is roughly a third as many of them. | A crash leaves far less wreckage lying around it. The hole is the same; what came out of it mostly is not there. |
| `debris.settleShare` | `0.85` | `0.5` | A settled block is only a block change, but a wreck settles thousands of them and they all land in the same few ticks. Around half the writes. | A crash leaves a thinner heap. What is missing is gone rather than lying somewhere else. |
| `debris.maxScatterPerTick` | `96` | `32` | The ceiling that the two chances above compete for, so a single big landing cannot spend the tick on entities. | On a big landing the debris comes from whichever part of the hull was looked at first, rather than from all round it. |
| `debris.landingSearch` | `2` | `1` | The shell of positions a piece that could not be placed tests is 27 rather than 125 at worst. | A piece that comes down against a wall or into a hole has one block of room to find a home in, and drops as an item rather than settling if it cannot. |
| `shock.hullScale` / `shock.terrainScale` | `3.0` / `1.5` | `1.5` / `0.5` | A shock is a wave of block writes and debris spawns that one contact sets off, and a big landing sets off hundreds of them. Half the energy is roughly half the blocks each one reaches. | A hull that falls out of the sky comes apart around the impact rather than all over, and terrain keeps more of its shape under one. |
| `shock.kineticScale` | `1.0` | `0.4` | What a crash has to spend, per kilojoule the striking body is carrying. This is the number that decides how big a wave is at all, and a wave is block writes and debris spawns. Two fifths of the budget is roughly two fifths of the volume. | A hull that falls out of the sky is wrecked rather than obliterated, and the wreck is done coming apart sooner. |
| `shock.maxTicks` | `40` | `20` | How long a wave that outran its tick may be picked up again. Halved, so the tail a big crash leaves behind costs one second of ticks rather than two. | A very large wreck stops shedding blocks with visibly more of itself left than it would have kept on `release`. |
| `collapse.depth` | `24` | `12` | How tall a column is read for the material under it, and the read is a block lookup a step. Half the lookups in the most expensive part of a collapse. | A column whose floor is on the far side of a hold taller than twelve blocks finds nothing, so a build with a very large open interior fails around its holds rather than through them. |
| `collapse.maxBlocksPerTick` | `2048` | `768` | The ceiling every collapse in the level competes for, and unlike a wave what it stops is never resumed. Roughly a third of the block writes in the worst tick of a landing. | Several builds coming down at once share it, so a second wreck in the same tick keeps columns the first one would have taken. They stay standing. |
| `shock.fractureCount` / `shock.fractureGap` | `2` / `6` | `1` / `3` | One seam instead of two, and it gives up after three blocks of nothing rather than six. A crack is priced to cross a whole build, so it is a lot of block writes for one contact to set off. | A wreck comes apart in two pieces rather than three, and a seam that runs into a large open hold stops there instead of coming out through the far side. |
| `shock.falloff` | `0.98` | `0.9` | The share of its purchasing power a wave keeps per block travelled. Distance gets expensive four times faster, which bounds the volume any budget can reach. | The damage is tighter around where the thing actually hit. |
| `shock.maxBlocksPerImpact` / `shock.maxBlocksPerTick` | `8192` / `6144` | `2048` / `2048` | The ceilings the waves compete for. A third of the block writes and debris in the worst tick, and a hard total on what any one crash may ever cost. | A very large crash gives up on the rest of itself instead of carrying on across ticks until its budget is spent. |
| `protect.maxPerTick` | `256` | `128` | The most blocks one build may lose in a tick to everything this mod does together. Halved, so the worst tick of a landing does half the block writes and takes twice as many ticks to do it. | A wreck comes apart visibly more slowly. It loses no less in the end. |
| `protect.maxPerImpact` | `3000` | `1500` | The most blocks one build may lose to one crash, and so a hard total on what any landing can ever cost. Half. | A crash that would have taken a ship apart takes a section of it apart. The far end survives, lands, and is its own crash later. |
| `stress.intensityScale` | `0.02` | `0.012` | The master dial for how violent a crash is under stress. Three fifths of the intensity is a shock that dies several courses sooner, and every course it does not reach is block writes and debris it does not do. | A hull that falls out of the sky loses a smaller bite. The shape of the damage is identical, there is less of it. |
| `stress.floor` | `0.05` | `0.15` | The intensity at which a shock has stopped. Three times higher, so the long weak tail of every wave — the part that walks a long way through material it can barely mark — is cut off. | A shock that would have taken a few last panes at the edge of its reach does not get to them. |
| `stress.maxScan` | `24000` | `8000` | Blocks one wave may look at over its whole life. Under stress a wave passing through what it cannot break is free, so this rather than the break ceilings is what bounds the walk. A third of the worst case. | A wave running down a long corridor of material too strong to break gives up before it reaches the far end of it. |
| `stress.glassReach` | `64` | `32` | How far the fragile pass runs. The fill is a flood through solid material, so halving the radius is roughly an eighth of the volume it can reach. | On a very long ship the windows go out around the crash rather than end to end, which is the most visible single loss on this branch after `debris.mode`. |
| `stress.glassScanBudget` | `20000` | `6000` | Blocks that pass may read, which is its real cost — it reads a great deal of hull to find a little glass. Under a third. | A pass through a build with few windows gives up sooner, so glass past a lot of solid material survives. |
| `stress.glassMaxPerImpact` | `512` | `192` | The most panes one pass may take, and each one is a block write and possibly a piece of debris. | A greenhouse keeps some of itself. |
| `stress.glassMaxRuns` | `2` | `1` | Passes one body may start per tick. The fill reaches the whole build from any contact, so the second one mostly re-reads what the first already walked. | A build split in two by the crash gets one pass rather than one per half, so the far piece keeps its windows. |
| `bearing.interval` | `4` | `10` | The whole cost of the pass is the box it reads, not the damage it does, so this is the one dial that halves it outright. A quarter of a second between solves instead of a fifth. | A structure takes up to half a second to notice that its legs have gone, so a collapse starts visibly later than the crash that caused it. |
| `bearing.margin` / `bearing.drop` / `bearing.rise` | `4` / `20` / `8` | `2` / `12` / `4` | The box around a region drops from 24x44x24 to 20x32x20 - half as many blocks read per solve. | The box sees twelve blocks below the region rather than twenty, so a pillar whose footing is further down than that is anchored where the box ends and never reads as overloaded. Tall legs on deep ground survive what should have taken them. |
| `bearing.rounds` | `8` | `3` | Each round is a full re-solve of the box. Three is enough for legs, then deck, then what the deck was holding. | A deep chain of failures finishes over several visits rather than in one, so a big structure comes down in three or four steps with a pause between them instead of all at once. |
| `bearing.regionsPerTick` | `2` | `1` | One box per solve rather than two. | A wreck spread over several regions is worked through in turn, so the far side of a long collapse lags the near side. |
| `bearing.maxPerTick` | `384` | `192` | Half the block writes the pass may make in a tick. | What this stops is picked up on the next solve, so a large collapse is paced rather than lost. |
| `fractureWander` | `2` | `0` | A wandering seam takes the block on its plane as well as the one it wandered onto, so the drift costs the blocks it wanders over. A saw cut is the cheapest cut there is and separates exactly as reliably. | Every break in a build is a flat plane. It reads as cut rather than broken, which is the one place this build looks like a game and not a crash. |
| `fractureScan` | `48` | `24` | Three lines of reads per cut, halved. Twenty-four blocks is still long enough to tell a mast from a deck. | A build longer than about fifty blocks can measure as square from a break in the middle of it, and then the cut is made across whichever axis won the tie rather than across the length. |
| `fractureFloor` | `128` | `64` | Blocks a crack may take before its purse is consulted. Sixty-four crosses anything thin. | A cut across something wider than sixty-four blocks that the crash cannot pay for stops where the energy ran out, and what it leaves is a groove rather than two pieces. |
| `fractureNeck` | `12` | `6` | The search for the weakest cross-section is a window of blocks read per candidate plane, and this is how many candidates there are. Halving it halves the reads. | A joint more than six blocks from where the build was touched is out of reach, so a long limb clipped near its far end cracks partway along itself rather than shearing off at its root. |
| `fractureNeckSpan` | `6` | `3` | The window is a square, so this is the square root of the per-candidate cost. Three either way is a seven-by-seven sample and about a quarter of the reads. | A cross-section wider than seven blocks is judged on the middle of it, so a hull whose strength is all in its outer plating measures as weaker than it is and the cut lands a little off the true joint. |
| `split.rounds` | `24` | `8` | Extra passes of Sable's connectivity search per damaged build per tick. Eight is still eight times its own pace. | A wreck of several thousand blocks takes a second or two longer to notice it is in pieces, so the two halves hang together briefly before they part. |
| `split.millis` | `3.0` | `1.5` | Half the wall-clock ceiling on that work per level per tick. | Several builds coming apart at once share a smaller budget, so the ones later in the list wait a tick or two for their turn. |
| `sever.interval` | `20` | `40` | Ticks between two passes over the same build. Half as many walks. | A wreck takes up to two seconds longer to notice which pieces it is in, so the halves drift apart visibly late. |
| `sever.volume` | `1048576` | `262144` | The largest build looked at at all, as bounding-box volume. The walk is linear in this, so a quarter of the ceiling is a quarter of the worst case. | A build over about sixty-four blocks a side is never walked, so the largest ships in the world are exactly the ones that keep flying in one piece after being cut in half. |
| `sever.neck` | `24` | `12` | The widest cross-section still treated as a joint. Half as many layers are priced, and pricing is the part that reads block strength. | A joint of thirteen blocks or more is the body of the build, so a hull hanging by a wide flat deck section is left hanging. |
| `sever.millis` | `2.0` | `1.0` | Half the wall-clock ceiling on all of it per level per tick. | Several damaged builds share a smaller budget, so the ones later in the list wait a tick or two. |
| `maxFallQuietTicks` | `100` | `300` | A build in a long fall is measured once for the whole of it rather than three times. Cheap already; free now. | Ground generated under a falling build goes unnoticed for up to fifteen seconds, which is longer than any fall inside the world's height. |
| `anchor.ticks` | `200` | `100` | Half as long holding the world open under a wreck. Five seconds still covers the collapse and the separations; it is the long tail after them that goes. | A wreck still shedding blocks after five seconds can be unloaded mid-collapse, which is the vanish this feature exists to stop. |
| `anchor.chunks` | `64` | `24` | Only builds up to about an 80-block footprint are anchored at all, so the largest ships never hold a wide field of chunks ticking. | The largest builds - the ones whose reload stall is worst - get no protection from it. |
| `anchor.builds` | `32` | `10` | Ten builds held at once rather than thirty-two. In a hangar the far ones are let go a good deal sooner. | An eleventh build, and everything past it, can be serialised out - which on a world full of parked airships is most of them. |
| `anchor.total` | `256` | `96` | Ninety-six chunk columns forced to block-tick across the dimension rather than two hundred and fifty-six. | Past ninety-six columns the anchor stops taking new ground, so a cluster spread wide is only half held - which is the same as not held. |
| `clearSoftBlocks` | `true` | `false` | A swept slab per axis, every `softSweepInterval` ticks, per moving hull. | Grass, flowers and vines in the path are left standing. They have no collision either way, so this is scenery only. |

## Beyond these

The knobs still worth turning for speed, in order: `bearing = false`, `stress.glass = false`,
`shock.shockBlocks = false`,
`crackBlocks = false`,
`debris.maxScatterPerTick` lower
still, `maxBreakEffectsPerTick`, `crushBlocks = false`, `sweepScanBudget`, `maxTickMillis`. See the
Performance section and the Debris chapter of [`README.md`](README.md) for what each one costs.

## Installing

The jar built from this branch is `create_aeronautics_impact-1.9.8-fast.jar` and lists itself as
*Create Aeronautics Impact (Fast)*, so there is no need to open it to tell which one is installed.

Same mod id and version as `release`, so only one of the two jars can be installed at a time.
